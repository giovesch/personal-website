Padova has more than twenty-five kilometers of vaulted arcades (*i portici*). For a photographer, these porticoes act as an infinite, naturally occurring light modifier: harsh midday Mediterranean sunlight is slashed into steep geometric diagonals and stark chiaroscuro.

## The Discipline of 35mm

Limiting oneself to a single 35mm prime lens removes the distraction of focal length selection. Instead of zooming with optics, you are forced to zoom with your feet, engaging with the spatial geometry of the city.

> "To photograph is to put on the same line of sight the head, the eye and the heart."
> — Henri Cartier-Bresson

## Light, Shadow, and Negative Space

In Piazza delle Erbe during the late afternoon:
- The shadows cast by the Palazzo della Ragione create sharp, deep black polygons across the stone pavement.
- A passing bicyclist or an elderly artisan illuminated for a fraction of a second against an underexposed background provides visual tension.

By metering for the highlights and allowing the shadows to plunge into pure obsidian, the photograph strips away clutter and reveals pure form, rhythm, and human posture.
